SUMMARY

period”). This action covers the portion of the investigative period since Scout became a Nasdaq
member, from November 9, 2011 through March 16, 2012 (the “applicable period”). While this

' ETFs are a type of exchange-traded investment product that are registered as open-end investment companies or
unit investment trusts under the Investment Company Act of 1940. Unlike mutual funds, ETF shares trade
throughout the trading day on national securities exchanges and at market prices that do not necessarily mirror the
net asset value (“NAV”) of the underlying portfolio of assets in which the fund is invested. Additionally, unlike
mutual funds, ETF issuers do not sell individual ETF shares directly to, or redeem individual ETF shares directly
from, retail investors or other market participants. Instead, only certain qualifying entities designated as Authorized
Participants can create and redeem shares directly with an ETF issuer.

? Leveraged ETFs seek to deliver multiples of the performance of an index or benchmark they track (by, for
example, seeking to replicate twice the daily return of the Dow Jones Industrial Average). Some leveraged ETFs are
‘inverse’ or ‘short’ funds in that they seek to deliver a return that is a multiple of the opposite of the performance of
the index or benchmark they track. See FINRA Notice to Members 09-31 (June 2009); SEC Investor Bulletin:
Exchange-Traded Funds (Aug, 10, 2012).
