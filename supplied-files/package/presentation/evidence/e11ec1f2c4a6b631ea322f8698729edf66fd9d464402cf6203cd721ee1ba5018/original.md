# Scout Trading LLC — Consolidated Investigation Findings

Each finding below is summarized in exactly two sentences, followed by its sources. Findings are grouped by entity and domain. Two entirely distinct companies share the "Scout" name — they are unrelated and are labeled accordingly.

---

## Entity A — Scout Trading, LLC (U.S. broker-dealer, CRD #151990)

**A1. Nasdaq Reg SHO naked-redemption settlement ($3M fine).**
On April 7, 2015, Scout Trading consented to a censure and a $3,000,000 fine in Nasdaq AWC No. 20100243866-01 for violations of Rule 204 of Regulation SHO and Nasdaq Rules 2110 and 3010, without admitting or denying the findings. The conduct ran from January 2010 through March 2012 and involved at least 255 "naked" ETF redemption orders covering 295,950,000 shares the firm was not long.
Sources: [Nasdaq AWC No. 20100243866-01](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/marketregulation/NASDAQ/DisciplinaryActions/SCOU_NQ_2015.pdf); [FINRA BrokerCheck — Scout Trading (CRD 151990)](https://brokercheck.finra.org/firm/summary/151990)

**A2. The mechanics of the scheme.**
Scout submitted redemption orders to its clearing firm while holding too few ETF shares to cover them, deliberately delaying close-out until just before the T+6 deadline allowed by the bona fide market-maker exception, then restarting the cycle the next day. The settlement states the strategy was "designed to maximize the number of days Scout remained in a short position for the firm's financial benefit," harvesting the structural decay of leveraged and inverse ETFs without borrowing shares.
Sources: [Nasdaq AWC](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/marketregulation/NASDAQ/DisciplinaryActions/SCOU_NQ_2015.pdf)

**A3. The ETFs and market footprint.**
FINRA's Short Sale staff reviewed 14 Direxion/ProShares leveraged and inverse ETFs (EDC, EDZ, ERX, ERY, FAS, FAZ, SDS, SSO, TMF, TMV, TNA, TZA, ZSL, AGQ) and found Scout's naked redemptions concentrated in 11 of them. In the ETF "FAZ" alone, Scout's activity drove roughly 95% of its clearing firm's fails-to-deliver and about 59% of all market-wide FAZ fails, keeping FAZ on the Reg SHO threshold list for 250+ consecutive settlement days.
Sources: [Nasdaq AWC](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/marketregulation/NASDAQ/DisciplinaryActions/SCOU_NQ_2015.pdf)

**A4. Supervisory failure.**
Scout had no written supervisory procedures at all — for either Reg SHO Rule 204 compliance or its ETF redemption/creation business, the firm's core activity. This absence underpinned the Nasdaq Rule 3010 supervision charge alongside the Rule 2110 just-and-equitable-principles violation.
Sources: [Nasdaq AWC](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/marketregulation/NASDAQ/DisciplinaryActions/SCOU_NQ_2015.pdf)

**A5. The firm withdrew before the fine landed.**
Scout filed Form BDW on July 17, 2014, withdrawing all broker-dealer registrations, and its Nasdaq membership terminated August 18, 2014 — months before it signed the April 2015 settlement. FINRA BrokerCheck today lists Scout Trading as a previously registered, former firm.
Sources: [Nasdaq AWC](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/marketregulation/NASDAQ/DisciplinaryActions/SCOU_NQ_2015.pdf); [FINRA BrokerCheck (CRD 151990)](https://brokercheck.finra.org/firm/summary/151990)

**A6. Related Wedbush clearing-firm action ($675K).**
On the identical facts, FINRA and Nasdaq jointly censured and fined Wedbush Securities $675,000 (accepted February 25, 2016; announced March 21, 2016) for processing Scout's naked redemptions without verifying share ownership and for having no procedures addressing Rule 204 or its Authorized Participant duties. Bloomberg Law described it as the first FINRA action against a firm for supervisory failures tied to naked ETF redemptions.
Sources: [Nasdaq AWC — Wedbush](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/marketregulation/NASDAQ/DisciplinaryActions/WEDB_NQ_2016.pdf); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/securities-law/wedbush-sec-to-pay-675k-for-supervisory-failures); [Mondo Visione (joint release)](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/finra-and-nasdaq-fine-wedbush-securities-inc-675000-for-supervisory-violation-2016321/)

**A7. Press and academic afterlife.**
The matter drew trade-press coverage in March 2016 and was later cited in peer-reviewed academic work on ETF settlement failures as a marker of the line between legitimate operational shorting and abusive naked redemption. Coverage appeared in ThinkAdvisor, Finance Magnates, and the Wharton Jacobs Levy Center working paper.
Sources: [ThinkAdvisor](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/2016/03/21/finra-nasdaq-fine-wedbush-on-naked-trading-in-leveraged-etfs/); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/institutional-forex/regulation/wedbush-securities-fined-675k-finra-nasdaq/); [Wharton Jacobs Levy Center (2018)](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/ETF-Short-Interest-and-Failures-to-Deliver.pdf)

## People behind Entity A

**P1. Michael Edward Bleich (CRD #4262671).**
Bleich, a Duke physics PhD and former Lehman Brothers equities trader, founded Scout Trading and served as its CEO and sole managing member, personally signing the 2015 Nasdaq settlement. He was never individually charged, sanctioned, or barred — the action named only the firm — and he remained active in finance afterward.
Sources: [Nasdaq AWC](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/marketregulation/NASDAQ/DisciplinaryActions/SCOU_NQ_2015.pdf); [MarketScreener — Bleich profile](https://in.marketscreener.com/insider/MICHAEL-BLEICH-A1P36J/)

**P2. Bleich's later firms — Ophir, Inferent, Entrypoint.**
After Scout, Bleich ran Ophir Partners LLC (2012–2017), worked at Inferent Capital (2018–2020), and is currently Chief Investment Officer and a control person at Entrypoint Capital LLC (since July 2023). Both Ophir and Entrypoint carry clean SEC adviser disciplinary records with no reportable regulatory, civil, or criminal events.
Sources: [MarketScreener](https://in.marketscreener.com/insider/MICHAEL-BLEICH-A1P36J/); [SEC IAPD — Entrypoint (CRD 313946)](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/firm/summary/313946); [SEC IAPD](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov)

**P3. Other Scout personnel.**
Named associated persons included Alexander Herbert Mack (CCO/FINOP, CRD #4805607), Wendy Katherine Haley (registered broker, CRD #4665200), and Robin Isidor Weisberg (high-frequency trader 2010–2011, CRD #2773885, now at Jefferies). FINCH LLC is listed as the firm's direct "member"/owner in BrokerCheck.
Sources: [FINRA BrokerCheck (CRD 151990)](https://brokercheck.finra.org/firm/summary/151990)

**P4. Dror "Drew" Niv (FXCM) — Ophir co-owner, context only.**
Niv, co-owner of Bleich's Ophir Partners, was separately subject to a 2017 CFTC enforcement action that fined FXCM and permanently banned him from CFTC registration, and later settled a related securities class action for $6.5M. These matters relate solely to FXCM and do not implicate Scout, Ophir, or Entrypoint.
Sources: [CFTC Order No. 7528-17](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/7528-17); [FX News Group (class-action settlement)](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/retail-forex/drew-niv-william-ahdout-reach-6-5m-settlement-with-fxcm-investors-in-securities-class-action/)

**P5. FINCH LLC ownership — confirmed owner, unresolved principals.**
FINCH LLC is confirmed as the sole listed "member"/direct owner of Scout Trading per FINRA BrokerCheck, but it holds no CRD and appears in no SEC filing. It was ruled out as a New York entity (zero matches across NY Dept. of State datasets) and is most likely a Delaware LLC controlled by Bleich, though Delaware's registry conceals member names — leaving final ownership unconfirmed.
Sources: [FINRA BrokerCheck (CRD 151990)](https://brokercheck.finra.org/firm/summary/151990); [NY DOS Active Corporations dataset](https://data.ny.gov/resource/n9v6-gdp6.json)

## Entrypoint disclosure question (current SEC angle)

**E1. Entrypoint's Form ADV discloses nothing.**
Entrypoint Capital LLC's Form ADV (most recently filed March 30, 2026) answers every Item 11 disciplinary question "No," with all three disclosure-reporting pages reading "No Information Filed," while disclosing Bleich as CIO, control person, and 25–50% indirect owner. Whether the $3M Scout fine should have been disclosed is a genuine but contested question.
Sources: [SEC IAPD — Entrypoint (CRD 313946)](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/firm/summary/313946); [SEC Form ADV instructions](https://www.sec.gov/about/forms/formadv.pdf)

**E2. Strength of the non-disclosure theory.**
The literal Form ADV Part 1, Item 11 theory is weak because the Scout action named only the firm (not Bleich) and dissolved Scout is not an "advisory affiliate" of Entrypoint. The viable theory is the Part 2A, Item 9 materiality catch-all — moderate strength — offset by the 10-year look-back on the April 2015 event lapsing around April 2025.
Sources: [SEC Form ADV instructions](https://www.sec.gov/about/forms/formadv.pdf); [Nasdaq AWC](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/marketregulation/NASDAQ/DisciplinaryActions/SCOU_NQ_2015.pdf)

## Entity B — AC Scout Trading, LLC (commodities; UNRELATED to Entity A)

**B1. The LME tin-futures dispute and ~$27.7M loss.**
AC Scout Trading, a commodities trading vehicle, gave Ebullio Capital Management full discretionary authority over an LME tin-futures sub-account at SG Americas/Newedge, and claimed roughly $27.7 million in losses after a July 2009 trade collapsed. AC Scout alleged the broker concealed an LME warning that Ebullio's strategy would fail; the Illinois Appellate Court (2017) reversed lower-court stays and sent the dispute to arbitration.
Sources: [Illinois Appellate Court — 2017 IL App (1st) 160436-U](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/files/1160436_R23.pdf/opinion)

**B2. The FINRA arbitration outcome — AC Scout lost completely.**
In FINRA case 15-01201, a three-arbitrator panel issued an award on July 24, 2018 (after a six-day hearing) denying AC Scout's claims in their entirety — rejecting its $27,721,000 compensatory demand, $10,000,000 punitive demand, and all attorneys' fees. No petition to confirm or vacate was filed, so the defense win stood.
Sources: [FINRA Arbitration Award 15-01201 (PDF)](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/aao_documents/15-01201.pdf); [FINRA Arbitration Awards Online](https://www.finra.org/arbitration-mediation/arbitration-awards-online?search=15-01201)

**B3. Media reporting error worth noting.**
A January 2018 Finance Magnates article reported the "ruling went in favour of AC Scout against SG Americas," but it was published six months before the FINRA award and conflated the 2017 procedural win on arbitrability with the merits outcome. The actual merits award went against AC Scout.
Sources: [Finance Magnates (Ebullio investigation)](https://www.financemagnates.com/institutional-forex/regulation/ebullio-restructures-leaving-trail-enraged-investors/); [FINRA Arbitration Award 15-01201](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/aao_documents/15-01201.pdf)

**B4. The Ebullio tin corner and collapse.**
Ebullio Capital Management, the UK fund run by Lars Steffensen that controlled AC Scout's tin trades, reportedly amassed over 90% of LME tin warrants by late 2009 before its position collapsed roughly 96% peak-to-trough. Ebullio was later wound up amid extensive fraud allegations and was finally dissolved in September 2023.
Sources: [Fastmarkets — LME tin squeeze](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/how-the-london-tin-market-sleepwalked-into-a-once-in-a-lifetime-spread-squeeze-corrected/); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/institutional-forex/regulation/ebullio-restructures-leaving-trail-enraged-investors/); [UK Companies House — Ebullio filing history](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/OC331213/filing-history)

**B5. Confirmed: AC Scout and Scout Trading (CRD 151990) are unrelated.**
The two companies share only a name — AC Scout is a commodities/LME trading vehicle with no broker-dealer registration, while Scout Trading was an ETF market-making broker-dealer; they share no personnel, business, counterparties, or regulatory footprint. The name overlap is coincidental and the entities must never be conflated.
Sources: [Illinois Appellate Court](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/files/1160436_R23.pdf/opinion); [FINRA BrokerCheck (CRD 151990)](https://brokercheck.finra.org/firm/summary/151990)
