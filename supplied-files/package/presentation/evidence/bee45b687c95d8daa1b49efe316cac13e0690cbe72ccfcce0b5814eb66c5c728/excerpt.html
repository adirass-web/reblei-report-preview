Under New York Civil Rights Law § 76-a(1)(a), an "action involving public petition and

The "Substantial Basis in Law" Threshold and Discovery Stay 
Once the defendants demonstrate that the lawsuit targets protected petitioning conduct, 
CPLR 3211(g)(1) triggers a specialized, highly aggressive burden-shifting regime.1 The statute 
mandates that the court "shall" dismiss the complaint unless the plaintiff (Bleich) can

Appellate jurisprudence, specifically the First Department’s ruling in Reeves v Associated 
Newspapers, Ltd., clarifies that this is a "proof-sensitive inquiry" that is far more exacting than

disproves a core factual assertion, the claim inherently fails the substantial-basis test.1 
Furthermore, filing the CPLR 3211(g) motion triggers an automatic, statutory stay of "all 
discovery, pending hearings, and motions".1 This critical procedural mechanism immediately
