## Date and pagination correction

The PDF has seven physical pages but its substantive award is labeled pages 1 through 5. Physical pages 5, 6 and 7 are differing signature counterparts of Award page 5. They are not byte-identical duplicates and should not be described as three additional substantive pages.

**November 18, 2016 is explicitly labeled Date of Service, for FINRA Office use only.** The arbitrators' handwritten signature dates are November 16, 2016. Recommended wording: award served November 18, 2016. The interest order says date of the award; this source does not justify silently choosing the service date as that interest boundary. Preserve the operative wording unless another official record resolves the date.

## Firm identity join: supported inference with limits

There are three converging official record features:

1. The award names Scout Trading, LLC and Wedbush Securities Inc. and concerns a Clearing Agreement/PAIB Agreement.
2. Scout's firm BrokerCheck report physical p14 identifies Wedbush Securities Inc., CRD 877, as the holder of the firm's assets effective July 21, 2009. This page itself says holds firm assets; it does not itself use clearing firm.
3. FINRA's May 2016 bulletin p42 expressly identifies Wedbush as clearing firm for its broker-dealer customer Scout Trading, LLC, and reports the joint FINRA/Nasdaq $675,000 Wedbush fine. That settlement context is consistent with the arbitration counterclaim's request connected to a FINRA settlement.

These support a **probable same-firm join**, clearly labeled as an inference across official records. They do not make the award a CRD-linked source or identify Michael Edward Bleich as a litigant, witness, decision-maker or individual recipient of the award. Plausible unresolved alternatives include another same-name legal entity or a different dispute/settlement involving the same commercial parties; the award itself lacks unique firm identifiers and a regulatory case-number cross-reference. Exact name plus counterparty plus settlement context is stronger than name alone, but still not an explicit identifier match.

## Regulatory and arbitration outcomes must remain separate

FINRA's bulletin p43 states Wedbush neither admitted nor denied the charges and consented to FINRA findings. It separately states Scout, a Nasdaq member but not FINRA member, consented without admitting or denying the charges to Nasdaq findings on April 7, 2015, resulting in a censure and $3 million fine.

The award's Member vs. Member classification and FINRA arbitration forum do not establish that Scout was a FINRA member. The bulletin explicitly says otherwise for the regulatory proceeding. The counterclaim denial does not vacate the regulatory settlements, clear Scout of regulatory findings, establish that the underlying trading was permissible or establish why the counterclaim failed. The award does not purport to adjudicate Bleich's individual conduct.

The award orders payment; it does not prove payment, satisfaction, court confirmation, enforceability in a later proceeding or the absence of a later challenge. Avoid saying Scout received/collected the damages. The award is a forum decision on submitted issues, not an independent judicial finding in a court action.
